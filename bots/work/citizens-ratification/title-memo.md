# Memo: Proper Use of Officer Titles — Citizens Ratification Inc. (Texas nonprofit corporation)

**LEGAL INFORMATION, NOT LEGAL ADVICE.** Research summary of primary sources for corporate
recordkeeping and accurate self-representation. Not tax advice and not a tax-filing recommendation.
No filings were made or changed; no third party was contacted. Every final title and filing judgment
belongs to the corporation's corporate attorney.

## 1. Bottom line

Three separate vocabularies are being blended together:

| Concept | Source | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| "officer," "president," "secretary" | Tex. Bus. Orgs. Code (BOC) ch. 1, 22 | Actual corporate **titles/offices** |
| "responsible individual" | Tex. Tax Code 111.016(d)(1) | A **liability category**, not a title |
| "responsible party" | IRS Form SS-4, line 7a | An **EIN record field** naming a person, not a title |

**"Responsible officer" is not a Texas statutory title, and not a Comptroller reporting label.**
It does not appear in BOC ch. 1 (definitions), ch. 3 (formation/governance/officers), or ch. 22
(nonprofit corporations), nor in Tax Code 171.203 (Public Information Report). It appears to be a
conflation of three real labels: the IRS's "responsible party," Texas's "responsible individual,"
and the Secretary of State's own signature line, "Signature of authorized officer" (Form 802).

## 2. Texas corporate titles — exact text

- **BOC 22.231(a)**: "The officers of a corporation **shall include a president and a secretary** and
  may include one or more vice presidents, a treasurer, and other officers and assistant officers as
  considered necessary. **Any two or more offices, other than the offices of president and secretary,
  may be held by the same individual.**" (as amended by Acts 2025, 89th Leg., R.S., Ch. 199 (S.B. 2411),
  Sec. 41, eff. Sept. 1, 2025)
- **BOC 22.231(c)**: "The officers of a corporation **may be designated by other or additional titles**
  as provided by the certificate of formation or bylaws of the corporation."
- **BOC 22.232(a)**: officers "shall be elected or appointed at the time, in the manner, and for the
  terms prescribed by the certificate of formation or bylaws… **The term of an officer may not exceed
  three years.**"
- **BOC 1.002(61) "Officer"**: "an individual elected, appointed, or designated as an officer of an
  entity by the entity's governing authority or under the entity's governing documents."
- **BOC 1.002(70) "President"**: "(A) the individual designated as president of an entity under the
  entity's governing documents; or (B) **the officer or committee of persons authorized to perform the
  functions of the principal executive officer of an entity without regard to the designated name**
  of the officer or committee."
- **BOC 1.002(52) "Managerial official"**: "an officer or a governing person."
- **BOC 1.002(35)(B)**: "governing authority"… "does not include an officer who is acting in the
  capacity of an officer." (Officer ≠ director.)
- **BOC 3.103(c)**: "A person may simultaneously hold any two or more offices of an entity unless
  prohibited by this code or the governing documents of the entity."

**"CEO" / "Chief Executive Officer" is nowhere defined in the BOC.** It is a permitted optional
bylaws title under 22.231(c). Under 1.002(70)(B), whoever performs the principal-executive function
is the "president" for Code purposes regardless of the name used.

### CEO vs. President — combined, separate, required?
- **Can be combined.** 22.231(a) + 3.103(c) allow one individual to hold two or more offices.
- **Cannot combine President + Secretary** in the same individual (22.231(a)).
- **President is required** (22.231(a): officers "shall include a president and a secretary").
  Exceptions: a properly designated committee may perform officer functions (22.231(b)); churches
  are excused (22.233).
- **CEO is not required** and is not a statutory office at all.

## 3. Where the title actually goes

### (1) Texas Secretary of State filings
- **Form 202, Certificate of Formation – Nonprofit Corporation** — has **no officer fields**.
  Article 3 lists directors ("A minimum of three directors is required"); executed by the organizer
  ("Signature of organizer"). See BOC 3.009 (directors, not officers). *A certificate of formation
  therefore proves nothing about anyone's officer title.*
  https://www.sos.state.tx.us/corp/forms/202_boc.pdf
- **Form 802, Periodic Report of a Nonprofit Corporation** — item 7: "The names, addresses, and
  **titles** of all officers of the corporation are: (**The offices of president and secretary must be
  filled, but both may not be held by the same officer.**)" The form pre-prints "Officer Title
  **President**", "Officer Title **Secretary**", plus one blank "Officer Title." Execution line:
  "**Signature of authorized officer**."
  https://www.sos.state.tx.us/corp/forms/802_boc.pdf
- Statutory basis: **BOC 22.357(a)(5)** (report must state "the names and addresses of the directors
  and officers of the corporation"); **22.357(b)** ("**An officer** or, if the corporation is in the
  hands of a receiver or trustee, the receiver or trustee **shall sign the report** on behalf of the
  corporation"). Required not more than once every four years, on SOS request (22.357(a), 22.358).
- **Title to use:** the office actually held under the bylaws/board records. If the bylaws elect a
  "CEO" but no "President," the Form 802 President slot is a live problem — resolve with counsel
  (22.231(c) lets the bylaws say the President bears the title "Chief Executive Officer").

### (2) Texas Comptroller / Public Information Report
Framing note: a nonprofit that holds a Comptroller franchise-tax exemption is **not** in the PIR
population at all. Comptroller, *PIR and OIR* — "Entities Not Required to File a PIR or an OIR"
includes "**Entities that are exempt from Texas franchise tax**."
https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/franchise/pir-oir-filing-req.php
(Exemption authority: Tex. Tax Code 171.063.) Whether this corporation files anything is a question
for counsel and the corporation's own tax professionals, not addressed here.

If a PIR were ever in scope, the label is simply **"Title"**:
- **Tex. Tax Code 171.203(a)(3)**: the report must contain "the name, **title**, and mailing address
  of each person who is… an officer or director of the corporation… and the expiration date of each
  person's term as an officer or director, if any."
  https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/TX/htm/TX.171.htm#171.203
- **Form 05-102** Section A field labels: "Name | **Title** | Director (YES) | **Term expiration**";
  the signature block also has a "Title" field.
  https://comptroller.texas.gov/forms/05-102-a.pdf
- Comptroller: "The report must be completed and **signed by an officer, director or other authorized
  person**." (171.203(d): "An officer or director… or another authorized person must sign the report.")
- **There is no "responsible officer" label** on the PIR or anywhere in the Comptroller's PIR guidance.

The Texas term that does exist is a *liability* concept, not a title:
- **Tex. Tax Code 111.016(b), (d)(1)** — "**responsible individual**": includes "an officer, manager,
  director, or employee… who, as an officer, manager, director, employee, or member, is under a duty
  to perform an act with respect to collection, accounting, or payment of tax." Comptroller hearings
  hold that title alone is not enough: "Finding that a corporate officer is per se a responsible
  individual would render the quoted statutory language superfluous." (STAR 201806002H)
- **Tex. Tax Code 111.0611** — "Personal Liability for Fraudulent Tax Evasion" (officer/manager/director).
- **Tex. Tax Code 171.255** — "Liability of Director and Officers" after forfeiture.

### (3) IRS EIN / responsible-party records
- **Form SS-4, lines 7a–7b, "Name of responsible party"** — a named natural person plus SSN/ITIN.
  **It is not a title field.**
- Instructions for Form SS-4 (Rev. 12-2025), p.3: "The '**responsible party**' is the person who
  ultimately owns or controls the entity or who exercises ultimate effective control over the entity…
  (a) If the entity is a corporation, is the **principal officer**." And: "**For tax-exempt
  organizations, the responsible party is generally the same as the 'principal officer' as defined in
  the Form 990 instructions.**"  https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/iss4.pdf
- Signature (SS-4 instructions, p.6): "the **president, vice president, or other principal officer**,
  if the applicant is a corporation."
- **Instructions for Form 990**, Item F: "'**principal officer**' means an officer of the organization
  who, **regardless of title**, has ultimate responsibility for implementing the decisions of the
  organization's governing body, or for supervising the management, administration, or operation of
  the organization."  https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/i990.pdf
- Form 990 Glossary, "Officer": "a person elected or appointed to manage the organization's daily
  operations… The officers of an organization are determined by reference to its **organizing
  document, bylaws, or resolutions of its governing body**, or as otherwise designated consistent with
  state law, but at a minimum include those officers required by applicable state law."
- Changes: Form 8822-B within **60 days**; Treas. Reg. 301.6109-1(d)(2)(ii).
  https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/responsible-parties-and-nominees
- **Takeaway:** in IRS records the concept is "responsible party"/"principal officer" — functional
  descriptions. A genuine top executive fits. But "Responsible Party" is not a title to sign with.

### (4) Bylaws / board officer roles — the only place a title is *created*
- **BOC 22.102** (bylaws adopted by the board), **22.231(a)** (mandatory president + secretary),
  **22.231(c)** (other/additional titles), **22.232** (election/appointment; 3-year max term),
  **3.103(b)** (an officer's duties and authority are as provided by the governing documents or the
  governing authority that elects or appoints the officer), **3.103(c)** (multiple offices),
  **22.104(a)** (organization meeting: adopt bylaws and **elect officers**).
- Titles are only real to the extent the bylaws create them and the board conferred them.

### (5) Telnyx / TCR brand registration + compliance letter
- **TCR brand registration has no officer-title field.** Telnyx *10DLC Brand Registration* required
  fields: `entityType` (`NON_PROFIT` for a 501(c)(3)-or-equivalent), `displayName`, `companyName`
  ("**Legal company name (must match EIN records)**"), `ein` (`XX-XXXXXXX`), `phone`, `street`,
  `city`, `state`, `postalCode`, `country`, `email`, `website`, `vertical` (e.g. `NGO`, `POLITICAL`).
  Optional: `altBusinessId`/`altBusinessIdType` (DUNS/GIIN/LEI), `stockSymbol`/`stockExchange`.
  https://developers.telnyx.com/docs/messaging/10dlc/brand-registration
  Telnyx API `CreateBrand`: `companyName` — "(Required for Non-profit/private/public) Legal company
  name."  https://developers.telnyx.com/api-reference/brands/create-brand
- So the title matters **only in the compliance letter's signature block**, where it is a
  representation of authority that the carrier/CSP relies on — not a regulated data field.
- Identity fields must match the IRS record exactly: Telnyx troubleshooting — brand goes `UNVERIFIED`
  when "the `companyName` you submitted doesn't match what the IRS has on file for that EIN"; support
  guidance says match the **CP 575 EIN confirmation letter** exactly (legal name, address, EIN).

## 4. Which document proves Jeff's title

Jeff states he is CEO. Nothing here confirms or infers his actual elected title. Ranked by
probative force:

1. **Bylaws** — create the office and its title (22.102, 22.231(c)). *Necessary but not sufficient:
   they define the office, not who holds it.*
2. **Board minutes or unanimous written consent electing/appointing him**, with date and term
   (22.232; 22.220 action by written consent; **3.151(a)(2)** requires the corporation to keep
   "minutes of the proceedings of the… governing authority"). **This is the operative proof.**
3. **Secretary's / incumbency certificate** signed by the Secretary, certifying the bylaws provision
   and the electing resolution. This is the artifact carriers, banks, and vendors normally accept.
4. **Form 802 Periodic Report** on file with the SOS — item 7 shows officer names, addresses, titles;
   public, but accurate only as of its filing date and only filed on SOS request.
5. **PIR** — derivative and likely nonexistent for an exempt entity; do not rely on it.
6. **Certificate of formation (Form 202)** — **does not prove any officer title.** It lists directors
   only (BOC 3.009). Do not cite it for this.

## 5. Recommended signature line

All options assume corporate counsel confirms the bylaws/board records actually say so.

**A. If the bylaws create a CEO office and the board elected him to it:**
```
Jeff Gunson
Chief Executive Officer
Citizens Ratification Inc., a Texas nonprofit corporation
Date: ____________
```

**B. Preferred if he is the principal executive and the bylaws name that office "President"**
(also keeps Form 802's mandatory President slot consistent):
```
Jeff Gunson
President and Chief Executive Officer
Citizens Ratification Inc., a Texas nonprofit corporation
```
(or `President (Chief Executive Officer)` if only one office exists)

**C. If the title is unresolved at signing time:**
```
Jeff Gunson
Authorized Officer
Citizens Ratification Inc., a Texas nonprofit corporation
```
This mirrors the SOS's own phrasing ("Signature of authorized officer," Form 802) but tells the
carrier less, so A or B is better once records are confirmed.

**Do not sign** "Responsible Officer" (not a title in any source reviewed) or "Responsible Party"
(an IRS EIN-record concept, not a corporate office).

Also: use the exact legal name **"Citizens Ratification Inc."** as it appears on the EIN/CP 575 and
the certificate of formation, and identify the entity type ("a Texas nonprofit corporation").

## 6. Flags for corporate counsel

1. Do the bylaws currently fill **both** President and Secretary (22.231(a))?
2. Is the same individual holding **President and Secretary**? That combination is prohibited.
3. Was Jeff elected/appointed by board action, and to which office exactly (22.232)?
4. Have officer terms **lapsed past the three-year cap** (22.232(a))? If elections were missed,
   BOC ch. 22, Subchapter J (22.501–22.512, "Ratification of Defective Corporate Acts") is the
   cleanup mechanism.
5. Is the Form 802 on file with the SOS current, and does it match the bylaws/minutes?
6. Confirm the legal name/EIN/address triple matches the CP 575 before the brand registration is
   submitted or re-vetted.
